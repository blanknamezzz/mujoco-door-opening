\begin{abstract}
Reaching a door handle is only the beginning of a successful opening: the
chosen station must preserve arm workspace, the grasp must sustain contact,
and the available view must reveal relevant obstacles. We develop a continuous
MuJoCo system that connects inspection (I), positioning (P), and grasp selection
(G) through downstream physical outcomes. A shared H-JEPA visual encoder is
adapted with rank-8 LoRA and task heads. Grasp outcomes supervise G, the grasp
selected by G supplies P's target, and fixed P/G continuations teach I whether
an additional observation justifies its travel and time. On the latest held-out
72-scene test, the selected policy achieves 70 correct decisions, opening
50 of 52 candidate-feasible scenes and correctly refusing all 20 remaining
scenes, with zero observed collisions. It matches the task outcomes of always
inspecting while reducing inspections from 72 to 43 and mean episode time from
55.68 to 38.31 simulated seconds. Three disjoint test cohorts yield 210/216
correct decisions within nine scene templates. The results demonstrate
task-aware preparation and selective observation for a finite action library
in simulation.
\end{abstract}
