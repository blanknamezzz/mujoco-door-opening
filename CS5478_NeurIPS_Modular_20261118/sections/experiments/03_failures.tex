\subsection{Failure Analysis}
Cohort A contains four incorrect decisions. Three layouts in one geometry
group lead to attempted continuations that end with
\texttt{handle\_not\_detected}; none of the recorded candidates succeeds in
those scenes. The fourth is a false refusal: the observed-depth mask excludes
a station whose recorded grasp completes the opening. Cohort C
contains two false refusals on candidate-feasible scenes. Together these
errors identify handle estimation and candidate exclusion as practical
bottlenecks upstream of final action scoring.

Physical diagnostics explain additional failure mechanisms. In
Figure~\ref{fig:joint_limit}, the elbow reaches $-163.59^\circ$ with
$1.41^\circ$ remaining to its lower bound; the next target is rejected while
both fingers still contact the handle. A separate low-friction diagnostic
($\mu=0.005$) raises a slip alarm at approximately 3.77~s and persistent
contact loss at 4.73~s. Its diagnostic continuation separates the initial
slip from the subsequent release. These traces motivate monitoring joint
margin and contact state throughout opening, in addition to predicting the
initial grasp outcome.
